Claude Code is great in the terminal, but a lot of my day happens in apps that have no command line:
Mail, System Settings, web dashboards, the occasional ancient admin panel. Built-in computer use isn't
available on my plan, so I built my own: **CU mac**, a menu bar app plus a `cu` command that Claude
Code can call to take screenshots, click, type and scroll.

The interesting part wasn't the clicking. It was making it feel safe to let an AI do that on the
machine I actually work on:

- **Always visible.** While the AI works, a glowing ring runs around its screen, and a small note
  narrates every step ("Opening Mail", "Drafting reply").
- **Esc stops it.** One key, any time. Afterwards it has to ask before continuing, and you can tell
  it not to ask again for the rest of the task. Play/pause gives you a minute of quiet.
- **You keep working.** It waits until you stop typing, acts, then hands your app and mouse straight back.
- **One screen.** With an external display, you pick the screen the AI may use. Yours stays yours.
- **Limits per app and per website**: Hidden, Can only look, Can click, Full control. Password
  managers are hidden by default, and setup suggests hiding banking and messaging apps. Only you can
  loosen a limit, and only with Touch ID; the AI can merely ask, in plain words, with the risk spelled out.
- **You sign in, not the AI.** When it hits a login, it sends a popup to your screen that takes you
  to the right window.

**Download:** [cu.felkru.com](https://cu.felkru.com). Free, macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon and
Intel. You'll need [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code); CU mac sets up the rest on first launch.

CU mac is an independent project and isn't affiliated with Anthropic. It's early, so feedback is very welcome.
